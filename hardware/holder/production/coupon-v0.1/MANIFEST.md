## Variants

All four have 3 arms and a Ø34 x 2.4 mm flange. Values are from OpenSCAD's echo
output.

- **Arm** is the arm thickness.
- **Slope** is the barb face slope.
- **Preload** is how far the arm stays bent in the loosest hole (Ø29, 3 mm wall).
- **Strain** is the arm root strain when squeezed through a 24 mm hole.

| Variant | Arm | Slope | Preload | Barb tip Ø | Arm top | Strain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (baseline) | 1.0 mm | 0.25 | 0.5 mm | 33.3 mm | 15.7 mm | 1.8% |
| B (stiffer) | 1.2 mm | 0.25 | 0.5 mm | 33.3 mm | 15.5 mm | 2.2% |
| C (steeper) | 1.0 mm | 0.40 | 0.5 mm | 35.2 mm | 16.9 mm | 2.2% |
| D (firmer) | 1.0 mm | 0.25 | 1.0 mm | 34.3 mm | 16.3 mm | 2.0% |

C's arms are too tall for the current full-holder board height. It is here only
to test the steeper barb.

## Print settings

Ender 3 Pro with a BLTouch (Marlin 2.0.x), 0.4 mm nozzle, Insignia PETG
1.75 mm, white (NS-3FPETG2WH), dried. Flange or flat side down, no supports.
The G-code already includes all of this. Copy it to the SD card and print.

Before the first PETG print, print `pid_tune_hotend_235.gcode` once (about
5 minutes). Marlin's stock hotend tuning is for PLA temperatures, and at 240 °C
the first coupon attempt stopped twice with thermal runaway errors.

Each print starts by heating the bed to 70 °C, then homing and probing it
(`G29`) with the nozzle held at 160 °C so it doesn't ooze. Only then does it
heat the nozzle. The first attempt, without probing, printed loose, clumped
first layers on the right-hand rings. `G29` assumes Marlin's bilinear bed
leveling, the usual BLTouch build. Unified Bed Leveling (UBL) would need a
different command.

| | Coupon | Test rings |
|---|---|---|
| Nozzle / bed | 235 / 70 °C | 235 / 70 °C |
| Layer height | 0.2 mm | 0.3 mm (3 and 9 mm are whole multiples, so the walls stay exact) |
| Walls | 4 | 3 |
| Infill | 40% | 15% |
| Line width (walls) | 0.5 mm | 0.5 mm |

Both parts also use:

- Seam at the rear.
- Fan 30 - 50%.
- A skirt and no brim. A brim inside the ring can fuse the arms to the flange.
- 0.2 mm elephant's-foot compensation.
- Arachne walls.
- Everything else from PrusaSlicer's bundled Creality presets: "Creality Ender-3
  Pro (0.4 mm nozzle)", "0.20 mm NORMAL (0.4 mm nozzle) @CREALITY" and "Generic
  PETG @CREALITY". These include Marlin flavor and a 5 mm Bowden retraction.
  The start G-code is Creality's "slowabl" bed-leveling variant.

Checked in the G-code:

- Every arm prints as exactly two outlines, with no gap fill. That's 0.52 mm
  lines on the 1.0 mm arms (A, C, D) and 0.62 mm lines on B's 1.2 mm arms
  (Arachne widens them to fit).
- The skirt stays outside the rings.

The test rings are gauges, not strength tests, so they use faster settings. That
cuts them from about 2 h 12 min to 1 h 16 min. The coupon keeps the slow
Creality wall speeds (25 mm/s outer, 40 mm/s inner) on purpose, since its arms
are what's under test. Faster walls only saved about 15 minutes.

Use glue stick as a release on PEI or glass. PETG can bond hard enough to tear
the surface. The filament's label says nozzle 240 - 260 °C, bed 60 - 70 °C. The nozzle
runs at 235 °C, 5 °C under the label. That's common for PETG, and it leaves
headroom below the stock PTFE-lined hotend's 240 °C ceiling, where it tripped
thermal runaway. If layers bond poorly, go back to 240 °C after the PID tune.
Above that, the fix is a hotter-rated hotend, not a higher setting.

### Slicing

Run `slicer/slice.sh` (needs `prusa-slicer` and `python3`). It:

1. Flattens the three Creality presets from
   `/usr/share/PrusaSlicer/profiles/Creality.ini` into plain configs
   (`slicer/printer_*.ini`, `print_*.ini`, `filament_*.ini`), plus
   Creality's `*slowabl*` BLTouch start G-code (`printer_bltouch_start.ini`).
2. Loads them, then `coupon_overrides.ini` (and `test_rings_overrides.ini` for the
   rings).
3. Writes both G-code files into this folder.

The presets have to be flattened because PrusaSlicer 2.9's
`--printer-profile` option silently falls back to "- default FFF -". That gives
a 200 mm bed and RepRap flavor, the wrong printer. Naming any preset also
overrides a printer config given with `--load`. After any re-slice, check that
the G-code footer says `gcode_flavor = marlin` and
`bed_shape = 3x3,228x3,228x228,3x228`.

## Results

Tested 2026-11-16. The clip ring design doesn't work: no variant pushes straight
in by hand, even in the largest test hole. The README's creep and wear tests
(test plan step 4) were skipped, because the design has to change first. No
variant won.

### Print

- Insignia PETG 1.75 mm, white. Printed with the G-code in this folder: 235 /
  70 °C, BLTouch bed probing before each print.
- The first coupon attempt at 240 °C stopped twice with thermal runaway on the
  hotend (E1). Its right-hand rings' first layers came loose and clumped (no bed
  probing yet). Both were fixed by the current G-code and the PID tune.
- The test rings printed first, then the coupon. The coupon took 2 h 51 min
  against PrusaSlicer's 2 h 01 min estimate. The start G-code's bed heating
  and probing account for part of that, and the printer moves slower than the
  estimate assumes.

### Test ring holes

| Ring | Designed | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| 24 x 3 | Ø24 | Ø24.0 |
| 24 x 9 | Ø24 | Ø23.25 |
| 29 x 3 | Ø29 | Ø28.27 |
| 29 x 9 | Ø29 | Ø28.3 |

Printed holes came out 0 - 0.75 mm small, and not by a fixed amount: the
24 x 3 ring came out true but the 29 x 3 ring didn't. The walls measured on
size (3 mm on the 29 x 3).

### Fit

| Ring | A (baseline) | B (stiffer) | C (steeper barb) | D (more preload) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29 x 3 | Only at an angle, one arm at a time | Same as A | Only with arms bent by hand | Same as A |
| 29 x 9 | Goes in; arms end up visibly bent | Goes in; slightly better | Doesn't fit | Goes in; slightly better |
| 24 x 3 | No | No | No | No |
| 24 x 9 | Skipped: same hole size as 24 x 3 | | | |

- **Grip in 29 x 3** (A, B, D): all very secure, more than needed. Pulling them
  out felt like it could damage something.
- **Damage:** after all the tests, no arm is visibly bent and there is no stress
  whitening.
- **Not done:** creep, wear, cable-weight hang, barb diameter after the tests,
  real houses.

### Why it failed

These are estimates from the design geometry and the photos, not
measurements.

1. **The barbs have to move inward too far.** Getting the barbs through
   Ø28.3 means about 2.5 mm per side for A and B, 3.0 mm for D and 3.5 mm for C.
   Through Ø24 it is about 4.6 mm. Seated in a 9 mm wall, the sloped barb face
   leaves the arms bent about 2.4 mm (A, B) to 3.3 mm (C).
2. **Curved arms run into each other.** An arc that curls tighter also moves
   its tip forward around the circle. Passing Ø28 takes roughly 25 degrees of
   that movement, but there are only 18 degrees between each arm's tip and the
   next arm's root. So the arms jam against each other instead of flexing. That
   also explains why no force gets them through Ø24.
3. **The load at the top twists the arms.** The barb is at the top of the arm,
   so the top bends in more than the bottom and the arm twists. The stiffer B
   resisted that a little better.
4. **There is more grip than needed.** Even in the loosest ring (Ø28.27 x 3,
   0.87 mm of preload for A), removal felt risky.
5. **The size range was too wide** for one snap ring: Ø24 - 29 x 3 - 9 mm,
   when the houses measured Ø25 - 28 (README "Physical constraints").

PETG itself was fine: nothing took a permanent bend and nothing whitened.

### Carry into the next design

- Avoid curved arms around the ring, or leave enough room between them for
  the tips to move.
- Keep the barb load from twisting the arms.
- Use less barb travel and less preload. Grip has plenty to spare.
- Size for the houses' Ø25 - 28, and expect printed holes 0 - 0.75 mm small.
